Recommendation

Semantic / section-aware chunking, target size ≈1024 tokens, no overlap

It posts the best MRR of all 27 configurations, ties for the best retrieval recall while using 35% fewer tokens per chunk than fixed-size at the same setting, and keeps chunk boundaries aligned to actual document sections — which also makes each chunk a clean, self-contained citation unit. It is also the only strategy whose results barely move when the overlap parameter changes, which removes a tuning knob rather than adding one.

B (recursive structural) is the pick if 100% evidence-completeness matters more than cost — it is the only strategy that never split a gold answer span across chunks, at a larger token footprint.

01How this was measured

Rather than judging chunking by eye, every config was scored three ways, from most direct to most end-to-end.

Evidence integrity — no retriever involved. For every answerable test question, does the exact gold answer-supporting text (from the benchmark's own evidence[].spans[]) end up sitting whole inside a single chunk? This is the most direct test of whether a chunking strategy is even capable of supporting a correct, grounded answer.

BM25 retrieval recall / MRR — a lightweight, embedding-model-agnostic retriever (query = conversation history + question) over the full 91-document chunk corpus, not just the required documents. Chunking's effect on retrievability is largely retriever-agnostic: a chunk that splits an answer across a boundary hurts BM25 and a dense retriever similarly. The eventual embedding model is a separate decision, evaluated on its own once it's chosen.

Context precision@k — of the chunks a strategy hands over, what fraction are actually relevant? Read this one relatively across strategies, not as an absolute forecast of require.md's 0.70 target: at k=5–10 the ceiling is mechanically low here since most questions have only 1–2 truly relevant chunks in the whole corpus. The production top_k (and reranking) is a later, separate tuning pass.
