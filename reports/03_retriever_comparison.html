aia-rag · step 4

The simplest retriever won

Chroma-backed dense retrieval (BGE-M3) vs. BM25 sparse vs. RRF hybrid fusion, then a reranker on/off pass on the winner. Two additions that "should" help — hybrid fusion and reranking — both made things worse on this corpus. That's the finding.

199 chunks, Chroma-persisted 85 answerable questions 3 retrieval strategies reranker: bge-reranker-base

Recommendation

Dense retrieval (Chroma + BGE-M3), no reranker

Dense alone beats both hybrid and sparse on every metric — recall@5 85.9% vs. hybrid's 69.4% and sparse's 69.4%, MRR@10 0.70 vs. 0.64 and 0.60. RRF hybrid fusion actually drags dense down here: BM25 scores 0% recall on cross-lingual questions, so blending its rankings in at equal weight injects noise rather than signal on a third of this test set.

reranker: not recommended — bge-reranker-base cut recall@5 from 85.9% to 70.6% and was 17x slower (66ms → 1,110ms/query). It didn't fail on the cross-lingual cases we worried about going in (those actually improved slightly); it failed on the ordinary same-language majority, which is what drags the average down. 01How this was measured

Same harness as steps 2-3 (src/chunking/evaluate.py): evidence-span containment against the test set's own gold answers, Recall@k, MRR, and Context Precision@k. Dense retrieval is BGE-M3 embeddings in a persistent Chroma collection (the winner from step 3); sparse is the BM25 baseline carried over from step 2; hybrid fuses their rankings with Reciprocal Rank Fusion (score = Σ 1/(60 + rank) per ranker) — the standard, weight-free fusion method, so there's no tuned coefficient hiding in the result.

02Dense vs. sparse vs. hybrid

Core retrieval metrics by strategy

All answerable questions, k=5 and k=10

Why hybrid loses to dense alone: RRF gives BM25's ranking equal weight to BGE-M3's, regardless of which one is actually right for a given query. BM25 scores 0% recall on the 18 cross-lingual test questions (it can't bridge scripts at all — see the step-3 report), so on those questions RRF is averaging a good ranking with a essentially-random one. Naive equal-weight fusion only helps when both retrievers are in the same ballpark; here they aren't.

05Choosing top_k

Recall keeps climbing through k=20 (multi-doc questions need two documents captured at once); Context Precision falls the whole way, since every larger k pulls in more irrelevant chunks alongside the right one. The crossing pattern is the actual top_k decision, made on this corpus's own numbers rather than a default.

Recall vs. Context Precision by top_k

Dense retrieval, no reranker · k = 1, 3, 5, 10, 20

k=10 looks like the right default: recall is already 96.5% (vs. 100% at k=20, for twice the context tokens), and it's where multi-doc recall crosses 94%. k=5 trades meaningfully more recall (85.9%) for not that much more precision. Final call belongs with the generation step, once real context-cost and the 10-second latency budget are on the table together.
